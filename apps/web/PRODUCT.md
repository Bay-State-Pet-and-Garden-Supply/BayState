# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Taunton-area pet owners, gardeners, farmers, and homeowners browsing the in-stock catalog. They know the physical store and expect the website to feel like an extension of it — same products, same staff, same grounded tone. Their primary job is checking what's available, not optimizing a cart for shipping.

Admin users (store staff, inventory managers, scraper operators) work in a separate surface with its own product-register override. They are not the primary audience of this PRODUCT.md.

## Product Purpose

Bay State Pet & Garden Supply is a family-owned local business in Taunton, MA since 1997. The website exists to put the in-store catalog online — same selection, same expertise, same neighborly feel. Success looks like a customer finding what they need and walking into the store to get it.

## Brand Personality

**Grounded, practical, community-rooted.** The tone is plainspoken and helpful, like a staff member in a green apron. No marketing-speak, no growth-hack urgency, no startup enthusiasm. The brand is confident because it's been here for decades, not because it's trying to prove something.

The site should feel like the uniforms, the signage, and the physical aisles — green, warm, unpretentious. Digital does not mean slick. Digital means useful.

## Anti-references

- **Shopify template farms** — cookie-cutter layouts, hero sliders with stock photography, "curated collections" language, generic trust badges.
- **Chewy.com** — big-box e-commerce polish, algorithmic recommendations, aggressive cross-sells, impersonal scale.
- **Generic SaaS cream** — white-plus-gray palettes, card grids everywhere, ghost buttons, soft rounded corners as personality.
- **Logo-first palettes** — the site is not a brand deck. The palette follows real-world recognition (green uniforms, green storefront), not the logo's color balance.

## Design Principles

1. **Match the store you can walk into.** The website is an extension of the physical location, not a separate digital brand. Colors, language, and photography should feel continuous with what customers see when they pull into the parking lot.

2. **Local, not algorithmic.** This is a community business in Taunton, MA. Avoid patterns that read as "optimized by a growth team." Real photography, real categories, real language. No AI-generated everything.

3. **Practical clarity.** Customers are here to find what's in stock, not to be impressed by a design portfolio. Navigation and information architecture serve actual shopping — browsing departments, checking brands, finding products.

4. **Green owns the room, burgundy remembers, gold whispers.** Palette hierarchy follows real-world recognition: forest green (~25% of surface) carries the identity, burgundy and gold are heritage accents (~10% and 5% respectively), cream and white hold the background (~60%).

5. **Practical over decorative.** The storefront's clean aesthetic (subtle borders, soft shadows, readable typography) is intentional — it signals reliability and ease of use. It should be easy to read and navigate without overwhelming the user. Every design choice that grabs attention should do so for a reason a store manager would recognize.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. `prefers-reduced-motion` honored throughout. No known specific accommodation needs beyond the standard.
